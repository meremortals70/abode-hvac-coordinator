# D113

People add vapour: occupancy is a moisture source the filter learns.

The room's moisture moves with the exchange to outdoors `h`, a steady indoor
source `q`, drying, the cooling response and the rebound. None of those knows
when someone comes into the room.

On Monday 23 November 2026 the living room gained **about 2.6 g/kg of water in
45 minutes** — its vapour pressure rose from 21.4 to 23.2 hPa while the
dry-bulb barely moved — and the rise began two minutes after the room's bed
occupancy came on at 21:38. In a room that size that is several hundred grams
of water: people, and whatever they were doing.

A threshold would not fix that. Asking the plan to cool harder when the
humidity is high is a rule standing in for a term the model does not have, and
it would keep standing in for it on a night with no people in the room
[D104]. So the term is added instead:

    de/dt = h (e_out - e) + q + q_occ [occupied] - drying [dry] - cooling x U [cool]
            + rebound [15 minutes after the compressor stops cooling or drying]

- `q_occ` is a **figure the filter learns**, in logarithm like the others, from
  the steps the room was occupied for and no others (`acting()` gains a column,
  so a step with no one there learns nothing about it). It starts at
  1.0 hPa/h and widens with the rest of the figures.
- Occupancy for a minute is the room's own answer — the grace-based flag
  `ComfortResult.occupied`, which the cycle has already decided. While a cycle
  runs that is the **previous** cycle's flag, so the source can lag the room by
  one cycle; a grace flag that moves in minutes does not care about 30 seconds.
- A save from before this holds six figures. It is **migrated, not discarded**:
  the new figure joins at its starting value with a wide uncertainty, and
  everything learned is kept.

**It is only priced where it can be told apart.** A room with no presence
sensor counts as occupied for ever (`comfort.py`), so on every minute the
steady source `q` and people's `q_occ` enter the same way and nothing separates
them. Learning anyway lets the pair drift: in a four-day replay with occupancy
pinned true, `h` was dragged from 0.50 to 0.365 per hour and `q_occ` doubled,
and `h` is what the forecast's decay and the drying response are built from. So
the figure learns, and the plan prices it, only once the room has been seen
**both occupied and empty**. Until then it stays at its starting value and the
source is treated as absent — which is exactly the behaviour before this rule,
so a room without presence loses nothing.

**The forecast carries it too.** A filter that knew about people but a
forecast that did not would leave the plan blind to where the room is going —
the evening rise would be corrected after it happened and never predicted. So
the plan takes the source into its six hours, assuming the room stays as it is
now (`PlanInputs.occupied` — the only thing a forecast can say about people),
and the trust check's forward run takes it the same way.

**Why the moisture side and not the heat side.** The heat balance already
carries a learned daily pattern — `g0` and two harmonics, fitted over weeks —
which is exactly a smooth representation of "people and appliances at the usual
hours", so occupancy heat is already in it. The moisture balance has no such
pattern: `q` is one steady figure for the whole day. That asymmetry is why the
evening moisture is the thing the model misses, and it is where the term was
missing.

**Not claimed.** No measurement yet of what `q_occ` learns, or of how much of
the 23 November rise it explains. The evidence is the shape of that evening,
and the test is that the figure learns only on occupied steps.
