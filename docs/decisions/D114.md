# D114

The plan expects the room's own hours.

Occupancy changes inside a plan's six hours: someone comes home, someone goes to
bed. The plan took the room's occupancy *now* over the whole horizon, so it could
not anticipate the arrival that adds vapour and went on pricing people after they
had gone to bed — both recorded as limitations when the term was added [D113].

The room now keeps a **share per hour of the day**, learned from its own
occupancy: one observation an hour, a running share that moves an eighth of the
way each time, so a week of days settles it and a change of routine shows within
a couple of weeks. Where an hour has been seen, the plan prices it; where none
has, it falls back to the room as it is now, which is what it did before.

Nothing is configured and no clock is branched on: the hours are learned from the
room's own presence, the room's own model of itself [D104]. The share is a
probability, not a decision — the plan prices `q_occ x share` — so an hour that is
usually but not always occupied is priced in proportion rather than assumed.

The loss is stated rather than hidden: a one-off visit, a holiday or a guest is
priced as the usual week. The plan sees the room's habit, not its calendar.

Status: in force. Cited in the code by `[D114]`.
