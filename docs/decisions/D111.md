# D111

A door to another room is an opening for the stop, and nothing else.

[D050] stops a room's unit once a listed door or window has been open past the
room's opening grace, with a warning first. [D105] carries the same openings
into the heat balance as air changed with the outdoors, `vent`, a + v o against
`T_out`. Both readings of the word "opening" assume the door opens to the sky.

An interior door does not. It swaps air with another room of the same house, at
a temperature the model does not have. Priced against `T_out`, the office on
16 November was told that three air changes an hour of *outdoor* air were
arriving through a door to the hallway — a false input to every forecast made
while that door stood open, which that day was six hours fifty minutes.

So a room names its doors to other rooms separately, and they are kept apart
from the openings to the outdoors:

- **They still stop the unit**, after the opening grace, with the same
  warnings. Leaving a room open to the house is still not a reason to cool it,
  and the stop is wanted as it stands: it is the backstop for leaving the room
  without pressing Leave, and for being called away longer than the empty grace
  (stated by the user, 17 November 2026).
- **They are never air changed with the outdoors.** The vent term reads the
  outdoor openings alone.
- **They are never offered as free cooling.** [D105]'s counterfactual carries
  the room forward with an opening open against `T_out`; a hallway is not that,
  so an interior door cannot make the advice say "open up".

A hall at its own temperature would make the door a third learned path,
`dT/dt += g · o · (T_hall − T)`, with `g` learned from the minutes the door was
open. That needs a temperature sensor in the neighbouring space, and there is
none in this house. This record therefore stops at keeping the door out of the
vent term rather than modelling it; a hall sensor is what would reopen the
question.
