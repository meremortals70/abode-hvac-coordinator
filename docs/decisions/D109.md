# D109

Dry runs on a curve of its own, and the plan chooses its rate.

A mode that removes heat is described by how much it removes at each effort
asked of the unit. Cool and heat have had a curve each since the model was
written; dry was offered at one rate only — the cooling floor — and credited
with cooling's rate there.

**The trial of 14 November 2026.** The office's unit was put in dry at a
setpoint of 18 with the doors shut and the room empty. Over 80 minutes the
room fell from 28.09 to 24.33 °C — **−2.56 °C/h** — and from 63 % to 43 % RH,
with the house load up 1.5-2 kW. The unit's own reading fell 28 → 24 against
that setpoint, an **effort of 6-7**. Where the plan's dry had run, at the
floor, the same room gained **+0.14 °C/h** (12 November, effort 0-2) and the
living room gained **+0.65 °C/h** (13-14 November, effort ~1).

| dry at effort | room |
| --- | --- |
| 1-2 — the floor, the only dry the component asked for | +0.14 office, +0.65 living room |
| 6-7 — a setpoint of 18 | **−2.56 °C/h** |

One share of the cooling curve cannot say both, and 0.10.9's single figure
[D108] was the wrong shape for the job: it was right where the plan used dry
and blind to what dry does when asked harder.

**Dry is its own curve.** Six figures, `rho_dry 1..6`, learned only from
minutes the unit ran dry, read exactly as cool's and heat's are:

- `unit_rate` for a dry minute is `r_dry(effort)`, not cooling's rate times a
  share;
- the cooling curve learns only from cool minutes, so dry no longer pulls the
  cooling floor down [D108];
- a dry minute teaches no `U_min`: what it delivers is not cooling;
- the plan offers dry at the curve's six rates, from its first step to its
  top, and the optimiser chooses between a dry rate and a cooling rate on the
  comfort index, the energy and the start cost [D029][D103];
- `delivery` sends dry the setpoint that asks for the rate chosen, read
  backwards on dry's curve — before this, every dry run was asked at the
  floor however little it did.

**Power is counted at the compressor, not at the rate.** A dry rate asks more
effort than the same cooling rate, so a power ceiling set in cooling rates
[I10][D037] would be exceeded if a dry rate were compared with it directly.
A dry rate is offered only where the cooling that same effort would buy fits
under the ceiling, and its draw is read at that cooling rate [D028]. The
demand forecast prices each action the same way.

**The starting steps are 0.4 °C/h each**, from the trial: 0.4 at the floor,
2.4 at the top, where the office measured 2.56. Every dry minute teaches it;
the figure is per room, because dry's capacity is the unit's, not the house's.

The state goes from 29 numbers to 34. A save from 0.10.9 drops its single
share and appends the curve; one from 0.10.8 and earlier appends the curve
alone. Nothing else the room taught is relearned [D031].

Status: in force. Supersedes D108's single share, and cites D029 for dry
being forecast against cool. Cited in the code by `[D109]`.
