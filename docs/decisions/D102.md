# D102

Never heat after a cooling cycle; never cool after a heating cycle (Jason, 8 November 2026: "there are two hard and fast thresholds. never heat after a cooling cycle and never cool after a heating cycle use pid to keep within the comfort band aiming for centre").

The cycle is the run. A running unit is not turned the other way: it runs its minimum, stops, rests its minimum, and the reversal counts and costs as a stop and a start [D016]. Limits holds the same guard on what reaches the unit.

After the run the plan does not reverse because the room has left its band, and it does not reverse to undo an overshoot. It first forecasts the room with the unit resting for up to an hour: if that rest forecast comes back inside the band, the plan rests and lets the room drift back. It turns the unit the other way only when resting would not return the room to its band (Jason, 9 November 2026: "It should rest and evaluate if the house will naturally move back into band, this is what should happen. if after a rest cycle it is not heading back into band then decide. but it should not reverse just because it has gone out of band"). A genuine need — a cold room on a cold night, a hot room in the sun — is still answered; an overshoot is not.

Two earlier readings are not in force. Holding the limit across the whole six-hour plan made the room fall below the band before it could heat, and rise above it before it could cool; each correction then ran at full rate and overshot, and on the whole-degree simulation the compressor started 10-13 times a day. Removing the plan-wide lock but letting the plan reverse freely inside the band answered an overshoot with the opposite run, which is the same fault at a smaller size. The rest forecast avoids both: the room is given the chance to come back on its own, and only a room that will not come back is turned.

Status: in force. Cited in the code by `[D102]`.
