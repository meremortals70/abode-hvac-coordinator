# D110

A meter on the outdoor unit's own circuit says when its compressor runs and
what it draws.

Some units report neither their compressor's frequency nor an `hvac_action`:
the house's Intesis-controlled heads are two of them. For those, the only
source left was the whole house's load, and it is a poor one. It cannot tell
one unit from another, it cannot see a compressor whose draw is small against
the house's own movement, and it cannot separate a fan from a compressor at
low draw.

**The night of 14-15 November 2026, office.** At 17:15:15 the plan's first step
was `dry 0.81`, sent as a setpoint of 23.0. The house load read 695 W at
17:14:43 and 694 W at 17:15:47 — the fan, and nothing else. It reached
1 205 W once, at 17:16:37, then sat at 726-841 W against a ~695-717 W resting
level until 17:25. The room's comfort index rose from 27.94 at 17:14:45 to
28.11 at 17:24:45 while the component reported it was delivering dry at
0.81 °C/h. The short-cycle guard held the stop for exactly ten minutes, from
17:15:15 to 17:25:15, because it timed the run from the command. In the same
cycle the plan's own blocked reason read "the unit stopped less than its
minimum rest ago: 0 min left": two modules disagreeing about one compressor,
from one snapshot.

A meter on that unit's circuit would have shown the compressor never started.
The command would have been released, and the rate marked undeliverable
instead of counted as delivered.

**One meter per isolator switch.** Each air conditioner's isolator feeds one
circuit, and one circuit feeds one compressor, so a meter at the switch
measures that compressor and no other — including on a multihead, where the
heads share the one outdoor unit and so the one meter. There is no breaker at
the isolator; the switchboard's breakers are sized for their circuits, so a
meter rated for the circuit is not a new constraint on the installation. The
reference device is a Shelly EM Mini Gen 4 per circuit, class: a clamp or
inline meter reporting power in W or kW as a Home Assistant sensor.

**The ladder gains a row.** `observe._compressor` answers from the best source
there is, and the meter is second only to a frequency sensor:

| Source, best first | How it is read |
| --- | --- |
| 0. head off or in fan-only | not running [D025] |
| 1. compressor frequency | running above 0 Hz |
| **2. the unit's meter** | **running at or above 0.15 kW** [D110] |
| 3. `hvac_action` | running when cooling, heating or drying [D025] |
| 4. house power | the load step, as before [D028][D101] |
| 5. otherwise | unknown |

A compressor's least modulation is several hundred watts and a stopped unit's
fan is tens, so 0.15 kW separates them without learning anything. The meter is
read on the same entity an `hvac_action` would answer for, and the source that
answered is recorded every cycle [D006].

**The draw is measured, not inferred.** Where a unit has a meter its draw no
longer comes from the house-load step at all. The unit's resting level is the
median of its recent readings below 0.15 kW — the fan, with the compressor
stopped — and its draw is the reading above that, learned at the effort of the
one head working. Two heads working at once cannot be told apart and that
reading is skipped, exactly as the load step skips it. Nothing is learned
until the unit has been seen resting, because until then its fan would be
counted as its compressor.

**The short-cycle guard times the run from the compressor.** A unit whose
compressor is seen running — by frequency, meter or `hvac_action` — is held to
its ten-minute minimum run on that evidence, and released at once when the
evidence says it stopped. Where the compressor cannot be told either way, the
command remains the best effort, which is what every house had before [D016].
The plan already asks the same question of the same answer (4.4); the guard
now asks it of the same source, so the two cannot contradict each other in one
cycle. The heads on one outdoor unit share its compressor, so the unit is
taken as running if any head on it is seen running, stopped only if every head
on it is seen stopped, and otherwise not known.

**The learned curves are recorded.** The mode sensor's `model` attribute is
too large for the recorder, so the learned curves never reached a history or
an export, and an assessment had to reconstruct them from behaviour. The
draw curves now publish as a house sensor, `learned_draw`: each outdoor unit,
each effort it has been seen at, the draw in kW, and the readings behind it.

Status: in force. Cites D016 for the guard's minimum run, D025 for the mode
guard, D028 for the inferred draw it replaces where a meter exists, D101 for
the load step's own watch, and D006 for the recorded source. Cited in the code
by `[D110]`.
