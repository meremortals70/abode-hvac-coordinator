# D116

A change from cool to dry prices the coil's moisture rebound as well as the start.

On the house's Intesis units changing from cool to dry stops the compressor for
two to four minutes [D103]. The coil is wet at that moment, so the fifteen
minutes of moisture return that follow a wet stop [D060] apply to the mode
change too. The plan charges that rebound: the optimiser chooses dry against the
humidity the stop returns, not against the dry curve alone.

The kind priced is the head's executed mode, not the last plan's proposal: a
short-cycle guard can refuse the change [D016], and charging the rebound then
would price a stop that never happened. The head's report is what the room sees,
so it wins over the component's own allowed step; the allowed step is used only
when the head reports no running kind. The same figure already decides the
reversal bar and the minimum run [D102], so those follow the unit's real state
too.

This is device-specific, and the asymmetry is measured. In the export to
24 November, cool-to-dry changes were followed within a minute by a 330–950 W
fall in the house load (Thu 19 twice, Sat 21, Sun 22, Mon 23, Tue 24). The two
genuine dry-to-cool changes showed no such step: one load rose 150 W and one
fell 190 W, dry's own lower draw. Dry to cool is therefore charged as a start
[D103] and its moisture is priced by the model as before, not as a stop.

Status: in force. Cited in the code and specification as `[D116]`.
